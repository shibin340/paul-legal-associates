Land Acquisition in Maharashtra: Understanding Your Compensation Rights

PPP
Adv. P. P. Polachan
Partner
|January 30, 2025|18 min readContent updated 7 October 2026
🌿

Landowners facing acquisition proceedings often don't realise the full scope of their rights under the 2013 Act. This piece breaks down the compensation framework.

Over three decades of practice before the Bombay High Court & Supreme Court of India, I have represented landowners — individual families as well as institutional holders — in proceedings under both the erstwhile Land Acquisition Act, 1894 and the present Right to Fair Compensation and Transparency in Land Acquisition, Rehabilitation and Resettlement Act, 2013.

One pattern remains constant: landowners rarely receive proper guidance at the notification stage, when the most consequential decisions about valuation methodology and objection rights are made.

Identify the Acquisition Law and Notification First

The project name or its proximity to Navi Mumbai airport does not identify the legal procedure. Read the actual notification, survey/hissa number, acquiring authority, statutory provision and award. Proceedings under the 2013 Act, a saved 1894 Act proceeding, a special acquisition statute, an agreed purchase and an MRTP Town Planning Scheme do not all have the same objections, valuation or remedies.

Maharashtra’s 2018 amendment provides for specified project exemptions by Gazette notification under section 10A, agreed awards under section 23A and distinct treatment of acquisitions under listed Maharashtra enactments. Check the applicable amendment, rules and project notification before assuming that Social Impact Assessment, consent, rehabilitation or a particular compensation factor applies. A project description alone does not establish an exemption.

The Compensation Formula — More Than Market Value

Where the 2013 Act’s compensation framework applies, section 26 sets the market-value criteria and valuation date. The First Schedule specifies a rural factor between 1 and 2 as notified by the appropriate Government, and an urban factor of 1. Do not assume every rural parcel has the same multiplier; obtain the notification applicable to the location and acquisition.

Sections 27–30 and the First Schedule distinguish the multiplied land value, attached assets and solatium. Solatium is an additional 100% of the compensation amount on the statutory basis; it is not a second addition of the unmultiplied market value. Section 30(3)’s 12% annual additional amount has its own statutory period. In the ordinary central text it runs from publication of the section 4(2) SIA-study notification until the award or possession, whichever is earlier. An exempted or special procedure requires its own applicable-date check.

That additional amount is different from section 80 interest where compensation is not paid or deposited by possession. Payment/deposit dates and the outstanding amount must be checked; interest does not universally begin with the preliminary notification.

We routinely find that initial award calculations by acquiring authorities undervalue the land by excluding comparable sale instances or by applying outdated circle rate references. A reasoned valuation submission should identify the statutory criterion, relevant registered comparables, assets and each disputed calculation. Whether an award can be enhanced depends on the evidence and the applicable remedy.

The Critical Objection Window

For land notified under section 11(1) of the 2013 Act, section 15 provides 60 days from publication of the preliminary notification for objections about the area/suitability, justification for public purpose and SIA findings. The written objection goes to the Collector, with an opportunity of hearing under the section. This is not a universal 60-day rule for every airport, highway, industrial or planning notice.

Preserve the notification and publication evidence immediately. Identify the provision, forum and last filing date before preparing a valuation or procedural challenge. Do not assume that an informal representation suspends a statutory deadline or that missing one objection stage automatically removes every other remedy.

  • Match the notice and plan to the village, survey/hissa number, affected area and your interest in the land.
  • Keep title/revenue records, service/publication dates, earlier representations and acknowledgement of filing.
  • Gather relevant registered sale comparables and asset/valuation evidence for the issue actually in dispute.
  • Obtain the SIA report where applicable, or identify the Gazette basis for an exemption.

When the Award Is Inadequate

Under section 64 of the 2013 Act, a person interested who has not accepted the award may apply in writing to the Collector for a reference to the Land Acquisition, Rehabilitation and Resettlement Authority. This is the statutory Authority route, not automatically the Reference Court procedure under the old 1894 Act. The grounds can concern measurement, compensation, entitlement, rehabilitation/resettlement or apportionment.

Section 64 has different limitation triggers depending on whether the person was present or represented when the award was made and on receipt of the specified notice. The ordinary periods are six weeks in the stated circumstances, or six months from the award where the section so provides, with the earlier expiry controlling in that branch. Limited further time requires sufficient cause; it is not automatic. Have the award, notices, receipt dates and acceptance/payment record checked promptly.

We have secured significant enhancements for clients at this stage, particularly where the acquiring body relied on stale valuation data. Past outcomes do not determine a new award. Rehabilitation and resettlement claims also require an eligibility and procedure review; section 64 expressly includes specified R&R rights, and Maharashtra’s section 31A addresses lump-sum R&R treatment in particular cases.

A Note on Infrastructure Corridor Acquisitions

For an airport-area or infrastructure-corridor matter in Panvel, Navi Mumbai or Raigad, bring the complete notification, map, title documents, possession/payment record and any award to the first review. Verify whether the matter is acquisition, a consent/agreed transaction, land allocation or a TPS before selecting a remedy. A neighbouring owner’s package or an advertised development opportunity does not establish your entitlement.
